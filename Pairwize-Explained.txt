PAIRWIZE, EXPLAINED
====================

WHAT IT IS
----------
Pairwize connects developers and brands with content creators in Nigeria —
priced in Naira, paid through Paystack, with money held safely (escrow)
until the work is actually done.

There are two shapes of deal on the platform:

1. BESPOKE WORK — a developer commissions a specific deliverable (one
   video, one thread) from one creator.
2. UPFRONT — a creator or brand lists a future program (a newsletter
   issue, a video series) and sells slots in it before it even runs.

Both follow the same basic money flow:

   Agreed -> Funded (held in escrow) -> Delivered / Program runs
   -> Approved -> Paid out

Nothing pays out until the buyer approves, or a set number of days pass
with no response — so nobody is stuck waiting forever, and nobody's money
moves before the work is real. And if something genuinely goes wrong
partway through, either side can raise it themselves rather than the
platform just hoping nobody notices (see DISPUTES, further down).


STAYING IN THE LOOP
--------------------
Every account gets a notification bell (top nav) — in-app only, no email
— that fires on the things that actually need a reaction: someone booked
you, accepted your offer, funded an order, submitted work, asked a
question, left a review, raised or responded to a dispute. Click a
notification and it takes you straight to the relevant order, offer, or
listing instead of just marking something read.

On top of that, three nav links carry a small "N new" badge — Requests
and Upfront for creators, Creators for developers (brands get the
Upfront badge too, since they browse it as buyers) — counting whatever's
appeared there since you last opened that page. Open the page and the
badge clears; it's a lighter signal than a notification for "something
changed here," not an action you need to respond to.


FOR CONTENT CREATORS
---------------------
List your rate once. Get discovered, booked, and paid — without chasing
invoices or negotiating in DMs.

1. GETTING LISTED
   Sign up and fill in your handle, platform, follower count, engagement
   rate, and niches. If you clear a minimum bar (1,000+ followers, 2%+
   engagement), your profile goes to a human admin for review — clearing
   the bar doesn't publish you automatically; an actual person signs off.

2. WHO CAN ACTUALLY SEE YOUR RATES
   Your rate card is visible to developer accounts (and admin) — not to
   other creators, not to brands, and not to anyone browsing while logged
   out. The point is stopping casual competitor price-watching, not
   hiding pricing from the people who'd actually book you. You always see
   your own rates fine when you look at your own profile; everywhere else
   the price is simply replaced with "Rates visible to developer
   accounts." Reviews and the Q&A thread on your profile work the same
   way in one respect — you need to be logged in (any role) to see them
   at all, though unlike rates that's not role-restricted once you are.

3. THREE WAYS DEVELOPERS PAY YOU
   - Rate card: you publish a fixed price + deliverable (e.g. "1 TikTok
     video — N180,000"). A developer sends a booking request against it
     with a written brief of exactly what they want — that's what you're
     agreeing to, and what delivery gets judged against later. You have
     to accept it before they're able to pay; nothing charges
     automatically just because they picked a price off your card.
   - Custom offer: a developer proposes their own price for something
     specific. You accept or decline.
   - Open requests: developers post a budget publicly. You apply with
     your own price; they pick who they want.
   All three land in the same place once accepted — an Order — and all
   three notify you the moment they arrive, with the developer's brief
   shown right on the pending card so you're never guessing what you'd be
   agreeing to.

4. GETTING PAID
   The moment a deal is agreed, the developer funds it — the full amount
   is held before you start any work. You deliver, they approve (or it
   auto-releases after 7 days if they go quiet), and the payout moves
   straight to your bank account. If either of you needs to escalate
   before that — see DISPUTES below.

5. UPFRONT TOO
   Anything with an audience can also list a future program and sell
   slots in advance (see "Upfront listers" below) — a second income
   stream on top of one-off bookings. Your own "Upfront programs" tab
   also shows every individual sale against your listings — who bought,
   what they paid, and where the payment stands — not just an aggregate
   slots-sold count.

6. WHY DEVELOPERS WILL TRUST YOU
   - Public reviews: every completed order can be rated — your track
     record is visible to any logged-in developer before they book you.
   - Q&A on your profile: people can ask before they pay. Answered
     questions build confidence for the next buyer too.
   - Reply-rate badge: once you've answered a few questions, your
     profile shows how fast you typically respond.


FOR UPFRONT LISTERS
--------------------
Sell inventory in something you haven't made yet — like pre-selling ad
slots in next quarter's newsletter, or sponsorships in a video series
you're about to film.

WHO THIS IS FOR
   - Creators with an audience who want to pre-sell a planned series
     before producing it.
   - Brands — companies with their own media property (a newsletter, a
     podcast, a digest) who aren't "hiring" anyone, they're selling their
     own inventory. Brands get their own onboarding page, kept separate
     from the creator flow.

1. LISTING A PROGRAM
   Describe it: title, niche, your audience (size, engagement), price per
   slot, how many slots exist, and when it runs. Like creator profiles,
   this goes to admin review first — judged on the details you give,
   since there's no automatic metric to check for a media property. A
   "List a program" button sits right on the public Upfront directory for
   any creator/brand account, so you don't have to already know it's
   tucked inside your own profile page to find it.

2. ONCE APPROVED
   Your listing appears in the public Upfront directory. Anyone can buy a
   slot — a developer, another creator, even another brand — buying isn't
   tied to a specific role.

3. GETTING PAID
   Same escrow logic as bespoke work: a buyer's payment is held the
   moment they purchase a slot. Funds release to you once the program
   actually runs (or the buyer confirms early) — automatically after 7
   days past your stated run date either way.

4. SAME TRUST TOOLS AS CREATORS
   Your listing carries its own public rating once buyers start reviewing
   completed slots, and has its own Q&A thread so buyers can ask about
   audience details or format before committing.


FOR DEVELOPERS
---------------
Find the right creator for your product, pay safely, and get real
promotion — without wiring money to a stranger on trust alone.

1. FINDING A CREATOR
   Browse the creator directory, filtered by niche, platform, and rating
   — every profile shows follower count, engagement, and (once they have
   any) reviews and reply speed, so you're not choosing blind. The
   directory itself opens on a clean top-5 "top rated" list rather than
   dumping every creator on screen at once — an obvious "See all N" leads
   into real pagination, and search or a filter jumps straight to the
   full matching set. New creators approved since you last checked show
   up as a badge count on the Creators nav link.

2. THREE WAYS TO BOOK
   - Book a rate card: see a listed price you like — write exactly what
     you need in a short brief and send the booking request. The creator
     has to accept it before you can pay; this isn't an instant
     checkout, it's both sides agreeing to the same brief first.
   - Send a custom offer: your budget doesn't match a listed rate —
     propose your own price directly to a creator.
   - Post a request: publish a budget and brief; creators apply, you pick
     who you want to work with.
   Once a creator accepts (any of the three ways), you'll see it in your
   notifications with a direct link to fund the order.

3. FUNDING & DELIVERY
   Once accepted, you fund the order through Paystack — the money is
   held, not handed over. The creator delivers, and you either approve
   (releasing payment) or request a revision. Nothing pays out until
   you're satisfied, or 7 days pass with no response from you.

4. IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG — DISPUTES
   Don't just go quiet and hope the auto-release doesn't fire on bad
   work: either side can raise a dispute (from the order page) any time
   after funding and before payout, with a short explanation of what's
   wrong. That immediately pauses the auto-release clock — a disputed
   order is no longer eligible for the 7-day sweep — and the other side
   gets a chance to respond with their own side of it before an admin
   makes the final call. It doesn't resolve itself automatically; a human
   decides, same as the original eligibility/listing reviews do
   elsewhere on the platform.

5. THE REST OF THE TOOLKIT
   - List your product so creators can see real traction before they
     respond to a request.
   - Post an open request when you'd rather have creators come to you.
   - Buy an Upfront slot to get your product into a program someone else
     already has an audience for.
   - Leave a review once an order is paid — it's what the next developer
     sees before they book the same creator.


FOR ADMINS
-----------
Nothing goes live without a human decision, and nothing that involves
money disappears with a click. Admin is where the platform is actually
operated.

1. THE REVIEW GATE
   Creator profiles and Upfront listings never go public automatically —
   clearing the eligibility bar only moves someone into your review
   queue. You approve or reject; that decision is what makes them visible
   to the rest of the platform.

2. STANDING UP ACCOUNTS
   Create any account type directly — developer, creator, brand, or
   another admin — with a password you set and hand off yourself. Useful
   for onboarding a partner brand or pre-approving a known creator
   without them going through self-serve signup.

3. FULL DATA CONTROL, WITH A SAFETY RAIL
   Every entity on the platform — users, creators, brands, products,
   requests, Upfront listings — can be edited or deleted from one place.
   Financial records are the one exception: an Order or an Upfront
   purchase can't simply be deleted. Instead you mark it DISPUTED or
   REFUNDED (developer keeps their money), or — the piece that used to be
   missing — RELEASE TO CREATOR, which resolves a dispute the other way
   and actually pays the creator out. None of these three call Paystack
   to reverse a transfer that's already gone out; they record what you've
   determined happened.

4. RESOLVING DISPUTES
   A dispute can now reach you two ways: you notice something and flag it
   yourself (unchanged), or — more often — a developer or creator raises
   one themselves from their own order page. Either way it shows up in
   Manage Data > Orders with the full thread inline: who raised it, their
   reason, and the other side's response if they've given one. From
   there it's a straight choice — Release to creator, or Refund the
   developer. There's no timer forcing your hand; a party going silent is
   just visible in the thread, not auto-resolved against them.

5. SEEING THE WHOLE PLATFORM
   The Analytics tab covers more than a snapshot now: total GMV and
   platform fee revenue, a 30-day GMV trend chart, status breakdowns for
   orders/purchases/offers (both by status and by which of the three
   pricing paths they came from), users by role, creators/listings by
   review status, product and open-request counts, and a top-5
   creators-by-earnings leaderboard — all computed live from the same
   tables your edit tools work on, not a separate snapshot that can drift.

6. ACTING AS ANY ROLE
   Your account carries a real developer, creator, and brand profile
   behind the scenes, so you can book a rate card, list a product, apply
   to a request, list an Upfront program, or buy a slot — the same as any
   real user — to test a flow end to end or directly support someone,
   without needing a second account. Your own creator/brand profile stays
   out of the public directory though — it's a working account, not a
   real listing. You're also the one role that always sees rate cards
   regardless of whose profile you're looking at.
